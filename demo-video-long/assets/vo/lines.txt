One click on Start a brief, and LaunchDesk gets to work.
It drafts every section in one pass, so the market, the roadmap and the money all describe the same business.
First, the pitch. A one-line summary, a viability score out of a hundred, and the scores behind it. This idea scored eighty-two.
Below that, the problem, the solution, the value proposition, and the key risks, written plainly.
The market section sizes the opportunity in plain numbers. Total market, serviceable market, a year-one target, and the growth rate.
Then the customer segments, and the competitors, each one marked by how big a threat it is.
The product section scopes the MVP feature by feature, and marks what ships first. It also suggests pricing, and the metrics worth tracking.
The roadmap splits the plan into four phases, each with concrete milestones. It lists the team to hire, and how much to raise.
The tech stack comes with a reason for every choice. Plus the architecture, a realistic MVP timeline, and the hard technical problems to expect.
Financials cover the seed raise, the breakeven month, customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, and three years of revenue.
These are AI estimates, not research. Use them to decide what to check first, then verify before you act.
Go-to-market lays out the launch strategy, the acquisition channels in priority order, and a plan for the first ninety days.
And finally, a landing page draft for the new startup, written for its customers. Download it as a single HTML file, and you have a starting point.
